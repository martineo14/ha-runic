Runic.io for Home Assistant · v0.3.1

Runic automation cookbook

One real-life automation for every Runic alert, using three example vehicles: Truck 3, Van 12 and Car 2. Each one reads the details Runic sends with that alert, such as the speed, the battery voltage, the fuel drop or the days left before a service.

Before you paste one

  • In the Runic portal, tick the alert type on your webhook endpoint, and create an alert rule for it in the Runic app. An alert type with no rule never fires.
  • Create a new automation, open ⋮ → Edit in YAML and paste. Switch back to the visual editor and pick your vehicle in the trigger: the device_id in the examples is a placeholder.
  • Replace notify.mobile_app_my_phone, lights, speakers and cameras with your own. The “You'll need” list on each recipe says what to have.
  • The map links open Google Maps on the alert's position, from the companion app notification.

Security

The driver presses the panic button

A driver feels unsafe at a delivery stop and presses the button under the dashboard. Your phone sounds even on silent, with a tap-to-open map, and the office speaker announces which truck it is, so whoever is nearest can call the driver at once.

Trigger
Panic button pressed panic_button
You'll need
  • The companion app on your phone
  • A speaker and a text-to-speech service
  • A tracker with a panic button wired in
alias: Runic · Panic button
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: TRUCK_3   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: panic_button
variables:
  map: "https://www.google.com/maps?q={{ trigger.event.data.position.lat }},{{ trigger.event.data.position.lon }}"
actions:
  - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
    data:
      title: "Panic button: {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}"
      message: "The driver needs help. Tap for the location."
      data:
        url: "{{ map }}"
        clickAction: "{{ map }}"
        priority: high
        channel: alarm_stream
        push:
          interruption-level: critical
  - action: tts.speak
    target:
      entity_id: tts.google_translate_en_com
    data:
      media_player_entity_id: media_player.office_speaker
      message: "Attention. Panic button pressed in {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}."

On holiday, the van must not move

You arm parking mode before a week away. If the van moves, it's a critical alarm while your “Holiday mode” switch is on, and a normal notification otherwise, when it's more likely a colleague borrowing it.

Trigger

      - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone

GSM jamming: treat it as a theft in progress

Professional thieves switch on a jammer to cut the tracker off before they take the vehicle. Jamming detected near the truck at night is your early warning: floodlights on, the camera records for two minutes, and you get a critical alert while the tracker can still report.

Trigger
GSM jamming detected jamming
You'll need
  • A camera that supports recording, and /media allowed under allowlist_external_dirs
  • Floodlights
alias: Runic · GSM jamming
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: TRUCK_3   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: jamming
actions:
  - action: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.yard_floodlights
  - action: camera.record
    target:
      entity_id: camera.yard
    data:
      filename: "/media/runic_jamming_{{ now().strftime('%Y%m%d_%H%M%S') }}.mp4"
      duration: 120
  - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
    data:
      title: "Jamming near {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}"
      message: "Someone may be blocking the tracker. Recording the yard now."
      data:
        push:
          interruption-level: critical

Driving

Accident: ask “Are you OK?”, then escalate

The tracker detects a crash. The driver's phone asks “Are you OK?” with an answer button. If nobody answers within two minutes, you get a critical alert with the map, and the home speaker announces it.

Trigger
Accident detected accident
Uses
data.alarm_code, position, waiting for a notification button
You'll need
  • The companion app on the driver's phone and yours
  • A speaker and a text-to-speech service
alias: Runic · Accident check-in
mode: parallel
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: VAN_12   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: accident
variables:
  map: "https://www.google.com/maps?q={{ trigger.event.data.position.lat }},{{ trigger.event.data.position.lon }}"
  ok_action: "RUNIC_OK_{{ trigger.event.data.id }}"
actions:
  - action: notify.mobile_app_driver_phone
    data:
      title: "Crash detected. Are you OK?"
      message: "If you don't answer in 2 minutes, we'll raise the alarm."
      data:
        actions:
          - action: "{{ ok_action }}"
            title: "I'm OK"
        push:
          interruption-level: critical
  - wait_for_trigger:
      - trigger: event
        event_type: mobile_app_notification_action
        event_data:
          action: "{{ ok_action }}"
    timeout: "00:02:00"
    continue_on_timeout: true
  - if:
      - "{{ wait.trigger is none }}"
    then:
      - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
        data:
          title: "Possible accident: {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}"
          message: "No answer from the driver. Tap for the location."
          data:
            url: "{{ map }}"
            clickAction: "{{ map }}"
            push:
              interruption-level: critical
      - action: tts.speak
        target:
          entity_id: tts.google_translate_en_com
        data:
          media_player_entity_id: media_player.living_room
          message: "Possible accident with {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}. The driver is not answering."

Speeding log for a new driver

Your son just got his licence and drives the van. Each time he goes over the limit on the Runic rule, a counter goes up and the speed is added to a list. You can talk it through at dinner instead of getting a notification for every one.

Trigger
Speed limit exceeded speed_limit
Uses
data.speed_kmh, created_at
You'll need
  • A counter helper: counter.van_12_speeding
  • A to-do list: todo.driving_log
alias: Runic · Speeding log
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: VAN_12   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: speed_limit
actions:
  - action: counter.increment
    target:
      entity_id: counter.van_12_speeding
  - action: todo.add_item
    target:
      entity_id: todo.driving_log
    data:
      item: "{{ trigger.event.data.data.speed_kmh }} km/h"
      description: "{{ as_local(as_datetime(trigger.event.data.created_at)).strftime('%a %d %b %H:%M') }}"
  - if:
      - "{{ trigger.event.data.data.speed_kmh | float(0) > 130 }}"
    then:
      - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
        data:
          message: "{{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }} at {{ trigger.event.data.data.speed_kmh }} km/h"

Refrigerated truck door opened away from the depot

The truck's cargo door switch is wired to a digital input. Opening it at the depot is normal. Opening it anywhere else, while the driver is on the road, can mean a break-in or a load spoiling in the heat.

Trigger
Digital input changed input_activation
Uses
data.input (the input's name), data.level (0 or 1)
You'll need
  • A zone called zone.depot
  • To know which level means “open” on your wiring
alias: Runic · Cargo door opened outside the depot
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: TRUCK_3   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: input_activation
conditions:
  - "{{ trigger.event.data.data.level == 1 }}"   # 1 = open on this wiring
  - condition: not
    conditions:
      - condition: zone
        entity_id: device_tracker.truck_3
        zone: zone.depot
actions:
  - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
    data:
      title: "{{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}: {{ trigger.event.data.data.input }} opened"
      message: "The cargo door opened outside the depot."

Fuel & maintenance

Fuel siphoned overnight vs. a normal refuel

The same alert covers a refuel and a sudden drop. A drop of a fifth of the tank while the truck is parked is almost always theft, so that's a critical alert with the percentage. Refuels are just written to the logbook.

Trigger
Fuel level change (CAN) fuel
Uses
data.kind (refuel or theft), data.percent
You'll need
  • A vehicle that reports fuel over the CAN bus
alias: Runic · Fuel change
triggers:
  - trigger: device
    domain: runic
    device_id: TRUCK_3   # pick the vehicle in the editor
    type: fuel
actions:
  - choose:
      - conditions:
          - "{{ trigger.event.data.data.kind == 'theft' }}"
        sequence:
          - action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
            data:
              title: "Fuel drop on {{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}"
              message: "The tank dropped {{ trigger.event.data.data.percent }}% at once."
              data:
                push:
                  interruption-level: critical
    default:
      - action: logbook.log
        data:
          name: "{{ trigger.event.data.vehicle_name }}"
          message: "refuelled"

Bonus · whole fleet

Any critical alert from any vehicle: the office lights go red

One automation for everything you marked “critical” on the Runic rules. The office light flashes red for a minute, so people notice even without their phones. It uses the general event, so it also covers vehicles you add later.

Trigger
Event: runic_event level: critical
You'll need
  • A colour light in the office
  • Rules set to “critical” in the Runic app
alias: Runic · Any critical alert
mode: single
triggers:
  - trigger: event
    event_type: runic_event
    event_data:
      level: critical
actions:
  - repeat:
      count: 15
      sequence:
        - action: light.turn_on
          target:
            entity_id: light.office
          data:
            rgb_color: [255, 0, 0]
            brightness_pct: 100
        - delay: 2
        - action: light.turn_off
          target:
            entity_id: light.office
        - delay: 2
Alert fields from the Runic Vehicle Monitoring API 1.7.0 webhook reference. Integration: github.com/martineo14/ha-runic. Entity and service names in the examples are illustrations: use the ones from your Home Assistant.
